**A key you generate can now present itself as an X.509 certificate.** New `keys::self_signed_certificate(key, &CertSubject)` makes a self-signed end-entity certificate (subject common name, DNS names, validity from one minute to ten years) and `keys::certificate_signing_request` a PKCS#10 request to send to a CA, both from a `LocalKey` of any signing or TLS kind and any of ES256, EdDSA and RS256; the key never leaves the process. `keys::x5t_s256` gives a certificate's `x5t#S256` thumbprint and `keys::jwk_with_x5c(key, cert_pem)` the key's public JWK with `x5c` and `x5t#S256`, refused when the certificate certifies a different key. `LocalKey::generate` now accepts `KeyKind::Mtls` (it was refused for having no JWK `use`); encryption kinds are still refused. CA-issued certificates stay in `server::security::x509_signing`. No new dependency (`rcgen`, `x509-parser`). Evidence: six new `keys::cert` tests that parse the result back with `x509-parser` and verify the signatures, three of them shown red by mutating the key-match check, the validity window and the Mtls admission.
