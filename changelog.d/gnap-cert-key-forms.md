**Breaking: GNAP client keys can now be a certificate (`cert` or `cert#S256`, RFC 9635 section 7.1), proved by `mtls` (KM7).** `GnapClientKey::jwk` is now an `Option` beside new `cert` and `cert_s256` members, exactly one of which must be set (a certificate key must use the `mtls` method and `mtls` needs a certificate key; anything else is refused), and `GnapProof` gains `client_certificate_der`, the DER of the client certificate your TLS terminator authenticated on the request, which the service compares with the certificate the key names (both forms reduce to one binding, `x5t#S256:<base64url SHA-256 of the DER>`, which never collides with a JWK thumbprint). Without a presented certificate, or with a different one, issuance and token rotation and revocation are refused. This crate does not validate the certificate chain or perform the TLS handshake: that stays with the integrator, as RFC 9635 sections 11.3 and 11.4 leave trust to registration. Literals of `GnapClientKey` and `GnapProof` must add the new fields; `GnapTransactionRequestBuilder` gains `client_key_cert` and `client_key_cert_s256`. Tests: both forms accepted with the named certificate and refused with none or another one, both forms of one certificate bind one identifier, rotation and revocation need the same certificate, and malformed certificate keys (no form, two forms, wrong method, bad base64, not X.509, wrong hash length) are refused.
